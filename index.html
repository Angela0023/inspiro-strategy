My Standard Lead Generation & Campaign Setup Process

This is the process I typically follow when launching outbound campaigns for a new client. It's a reliable starting framework that I adjust as we collect data and learn which segments and messaging perform best.

1

Company sourcing

I use Clay to identify companies that match the initial ICP, applying filters such as industry, company size, geography, and relevant market signals.

2

Company qualification

Before finding individual contacts, I verify which companies genuinely fit the client's offering. I review their websites, business models, operations, and available market signals. Companies are classified by fit and priority so we don't spend enrichment credits on irrelevant accounts.

3

Contact enrichment

Once a company is qualified, I identify the relevant decision-makers and other stakeholders through Clay, Apollo, LinkedIn Sales Navigator, and additional sources when needed.

4

Email discovery and verification

I find business email addresses using the available data providers, apply verification, remove duplicates, and exclude unsuitable contacts before campaign enrollment.

5

CRM checks and suppression

Before enrolling any contact, I cross-reference against existing customers, active sales conversations, ongoing negotiations, and opt-out lists. This ensures we never contact someone Inspiro's team is already engaging with.

6

Campaign setup and execution

I segment qualified leads into the appropriate email campaigns, configure follow-ups, coordinate LinkedIn outreach, and monitor replies and meeting conversions.

I don't automatically place every qualified contact into one sequence. The approach depends on the ICP, industry, buyer persona, and the specific problem Inspiro can solve.

Market Signals & Qualification

What to track

I track buying signals across multiple data sources to identify companies with active intent to explore outsourcing solutions. Each signal is scored and documented with source and date.

Priority A High-Intent Signals (75–100 pts)

  • Active BPO or contact-center RFPs
  • Hiring Head of Outsourcing, BPO Operations, or Vendor Management
  • Announced operational restructuring (CX or shared services)
  • Evidence of vendor transition or outsourcing expansion

Priority B Supporting Signals (55–74 pts)

  • Rapid customer-service hiring (validate intent: in-house vs. outsourcing)
  • Geographic expansion or acquisitions
  • New product/service launches
  • Seasonal demand increases
  • New CX technology adoption (Zendesk, Salesforce Service Cloud)
  • Appointment of new COO or VP Customer Experience
Qualification Framework (100-point scale): Industry/operational fit (30 pts) + Signal strength (30 pts) + Inspiro solution match (20 pts) + Buyer accessibility (10 pts) + Evidence quality/recency (10 pts). All signals, dates, sources, and qualification scores are documented in Clay for audit trail and messaging personalization.

Campaign Design & Messaging

A/B Testing

I test two approaches to identify the most effective messaging for Inspiro's ICP. Both versions use short, human messaging with a 3-email cadence (Day 1, Day 4, Day 7).

Version A: Simple Referral

Purpose: Identify decision-maker without immediate pitch

Subject: Customer operations at {{company_name}}

Hi {{first_name}},

I'm reaching out to find the person responsible for customer support and contact-center operations at {{company_name}}.

Would that be you, or could you point me in the right direction?

Best,
{{sender_name}}
Inspiro

Version B: Direct Context

Purpose: Identify buyer while introducing relevant benefit

Subject: Customer support capacity at {{company_name}}

Hi {{first_name}},

I'm reaching out to find who's responsible for customer support operations at {{company_name}}.

At Inspiro, we help established businesses scale customer service and improve operational efficiency. For one U.S. retailer, our work helped reduce customer escalations by 47%.

Would that fall under your team, or is someone else responsible?

Best,
{{sender_name}}
Inspiro

Follow-ups: Brief, non-pushy referral questions. No aggressive pitching. Contacts who reply or opt out are immediately removed from the sequence.
